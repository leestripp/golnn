Sensors:
* Boundry detection. A Vector pointing away from the boundry edge.
* Local Food Density. Point Food vector towards clumpped food.
* Scent Gradient Sensor: The world could contain a "scent map" (perhaps a 3D grid or a list of scent sources). Agents and food would periodically drop "scent particles" that diffuse and decay over time. The sensor would not see the source but would sample the scent map at several points around the agent to determine the gradient (the direction of increasing scent concentration).
* Auditory Sensor:** A simplified way to detect activity.
    *   **Implementation:** Actions like eating, attacking, or moving at high speed could generate a "sound event" at a specific location. This event propagates instantaneously to all agents within a certain radius.
    *   **Input to Brain:** A 3D normalized vector pointing towards the loudest/closest sound source and a float for its intensity. If no sound is heard, the vector is zero.
    *   **Benefit:** Feeders could be alerted to a predator attacking another agent, even if it's out of sight, triggering a `FLEE` response. Predators could be drawn to the sound of activity, hoping to find prey.
* Internal State Sensors: Provide the brain with more self-awareness.
    *   **Implementation:** Add normalized values for the agent's current `m_age` or a "hunger" metric (e.g., `1.0 - (m_energy / max_energy)`).
    *   **Input to Brain:** One or two additional float values in the perception vector.
    *   **Benefit:** An agent could learn to modify its behavior based on age, perhaps becoming less aggressive or taking fewer risks as it gets older to maximize its reproductive lifespan. A direct "hunger" input might lead to more urgent `HUNT` states than the binned energy level alone.

While direction to food and threats is a fantastic start, here are several other sensory inputs that could dramatically increase the behavioral complexity and "intelligence" of your agents:

1.  **Boundary Avoidance Sensor:**
    *   **What it is:** A sensor that provides a vector pointing away from the nearest world boundary when the agent gets too close.
    *   **Input to Brain:** A 3D vector. If the agent is within a certain threshold (e.g., 5 units) of a boundary, the vector points directly away from it (e.g., if near the `+X` wall, the vector is `{-1, 0, 0}`). Otherwise, the vector is `{0, 0, 0}`.
    *   **Why it's useful:** This would explicitly give the agents information about their container. They could learn to turn away from walls instead of getting stuck rubbing against them, leading to much more efficient navigation and exploration.

2.  **Local Agent Density Sensor:**
    *   **What it is:** A sensor that counts the number of "friendly" and "hostile" agents within a small, "personal space" radius.
    *   **Input to Brain:** Two scalar values: `friendly_agent_count` and `hostile_agent_count` within a 5-unit radius, for example.
    *   **Why it's useful:** This can lead to emergent social behaviors. Feeders might learn that a moderate density of other feeders is safe, encouraging flocking. A predator might learn to charge into a high density of feeders or avoid a high density of other predators. It's a fundamental component for collision avoidance and group dynamics.

3.  **"Scent" Gradient Sensor:**
    *   **What it is:** A more advanced sensor that detects trails left by other entities. The simulation would need to be modified so that food and agents leave invisible "scent markers" that decay over time. This sensor would provide a vector pointing in the direction of the strongest relevant scent.
    *   **Input to Brain:** A 3D vector representing the scent gradient.
    *   **Why it's useful:** This enables non-line-of-sight tracking. A predator could follow the recent path of a feeder, and a feeder could find a patch of food that's around a corner. This introduces a form of environmental memory and creates
4.  **Auditory Sensor:**
    *   **What it is:** A simplified "hearing" mechanism. Events like a predator attacking or a feeder eating could generate a "sound" at their location. Other agents within a large radius would "hear" it.
    *   **Input to Brain:** A 3D vector pointing towards the source of the most recent or loudest sound, and perhaps a scalar value for the "type" of sound (e.g., 1.0 for danger, 0.5 for food).
    *   **Why it's useful:** This provides long-range, omnidirectional information. A feeder could be alerted to danger far outside its visual range, and a predator could be drawn to the sound of other predators successfully hunting, potentially to steal the kill.
